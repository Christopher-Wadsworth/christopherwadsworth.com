Consider these potential start dates for Chicago history: 1673 Joliet and Marquette expedition; 1790s trading outpost of Jean Baptiste Point du Sable; and the 1803 founding of Ft. Dearborn; and the 1833 organization of Chicago as a town. Explain why each is a potential start date and then make a case for which you think helps us best understand Chicago’s past.

Throughout these four different potential start dates, each has its own pros and cons for considering it as the start date when looking into Chicago's past. While each has its own place to be used, I will evaluate them for how I think they would serve in a basic history classroom. For use in the classroom, I think that the start date should give students an understanding of how Chicago came to be what it is today, and why it is such an important player in the United States today. It is also important that what we call the start date makes it easy for students to reference, so they should have a concrete place and time to compare other things to.

By considering the 1673 Joliet and Marquette expedition as the starting point for Chicago history, you include all history of white men in the area, which started with the French. I find this date less important as there were already Native Americans in the area and their expedition could have truly been done by anyone. However, by instead calling the 1790s the start, based on Jean Baptiste Point du Sable’s trading post, the history starts at the first permanent resident of the area. This allows for the history to be more easily followed because it gives a point of reference of time and location that is consistent. Although, since he leaves after only a handful of years, this won’t be a clear place for students to connect other events to. In a similar vein, the founding of Fort Dearborn in 1803 serves as a good start because of it being such a pivotal point as it brought so many people together in the Chicago area with traders, farmers, soldiers, and cattle dealers. Jumping ahead thirty years to 1833 when Chicago was organized as a town is a strong contender due to it being the actual official start of Chicago as a city. This is a very good point to consider as it makes differentiating before and after Chicago easy as we have a clean date that it was officially organized as a town.

I think that of these four potential dates, that the 1803 founding of Fort Dearborn is the best for understanding Chicago’s past. For starters, this is when a community really started to form and stick around. While du Sable’s trading post marked a start for settling in the area, he left after just a handful of years. Second, I think that if we moved to 1833 or 37 when it became a town and city, this misses out on thirty years of important moments in United States history. These moments include, but are not limited to, the area’s role in the war of 1812, the expansion of John Jacob Astor’s American Fur Company, the Black Hawk War, and “the first known use of political cloud in Chicago” (Pacyga, 14). Lastly, I think that it serves as the best date because of how important Fort Dearborn was in all aspects of the United States during its run in history, with factors like war, trade, and regional control.
